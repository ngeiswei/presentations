It is not full on self-modification, the self-reference exists in the
sense that the instructions to modify the probability distribution use
to sample new programs are themselves sujected to these modifications.
So we have instructions that tells how to change the probability
distribution to sample instructions, including those that change the
probability distribution.

But the master program that does the sampling remains fixed, it is
never self modified.

And it was already very good, but he was obsessed with the idea of how
to allow full self-rewriting, not just on some parts of the program
but any part, and I mean, really absolutely any part of the program.
And I'm only guessing but I think he probably thought of something
like "in order to convince myself that a modification is beneficial, I
would need some kind of proof, mathematical or otherwise, anything
that can give me a strong sense of conviction" and I suppose at this
moment light stroke and he thoughts "well, why not have this
mathematician inside the machine!"  And thus was born the Goedel
Machine.
